Start here

Protect your data first.

If you only do one thing on this list, do this. A tested backup is what lets you recover from a failed drive, ransomware, theft, or a simple mistake. Untested backups fail exactly when you need them, so the test matters as much as the backup.

Back it up Automatic backups of the files and systems your business actually runs on.
Keep a copy off-site At least one backup that is not sitting in the same building as the original.
Test the restore Confirm you can actually get the data back, not just that a backup ran.
Lock down accounts

Make logins hard to steal.

  • Turn on multi-factor authentication for email and any key tools
  • Use unique passwords and a password manager instead of reused ones
  • Remove old accounts when staff leave, and limit who has admin access
Secure email

Email is how most attacks start.

  • Use professional email at your own domain, set up correctly
  • Train the team to slow down on unexpected links and payment changes
  • Basic spam and phishing filtering turned on
Keep systems healthy

Small maintenance prevents big downtime.

  • Keep computers, phones, and software updated
  • Run current security protection on every device
  • Monitor for the early warning signs that lead to failures
Network and Wi-Fi

A stable, private network.

  • Change default router passwords and keep firmware updated
  • Separate guest Wi-Fi from the network your business runs on
  • Fix dead zones and drops so daily work does not stall
Physical and camera security

Protect the building, not just the data.

  • Cameras covering entrances, registers, storage, and parking
  • Recording retention long enough to actually go back and check
  • See our security camera guide for coverage tips
Show up online

Be found and look established.

  • A fast, mobile-friendly website with clear contact info
  • A claimed Google Business Profile with correct hours and location
  • Consistent business name, area, and contact across listings
Have a plan

Decide what happens before something breaks.

The businesses that bounce back fastest are the ones that already know what to do. It does not need to be a thick binder, just a short, written answer to a few questions everyone can find.

If systems go down Who to call, what the backup plan is, and how to keep serving customers.
If a device is lost or stolen How to lock it out and change the accounts it could reach.
If someone leaves A simple checklist to remove access and hand off what they managed.

FAQ

Questions small business owners ask.

What is the most important IT step for a small business?

A tested backup. If your data is backed up and you have actually confirmed you can restore it, you can recover from almost anything: a failed drive, ransomware, theft, or a mistake. Everything else builds on that.

Do small businesses here really get targeted?

Yes. Most attacks are automated and do not care how big you are. Small businesses are often easier targets because they have fewer protections. Basic steps like multi-factor authentication, updates, and backups stop the large majority of them.
